13What are the fees and costs?

The main cost is the spread, the difference between the buy and sell price, charged on every trade. On top of that come the payment method's costs and whatever applies for holding a position open overnight. The current amounts are shown on the platform.

14What is leverage, and why does it wipe out accounts?

It means trading an amount larger than your balance, and it magnifies gains and losses in the same proportion. In practice: with high leverage, a move of just a few tenths against you already wipes out a large slice of the account — hence the risk-of-loss warning.

See how leverage eats into your balance
15Is Safirion regulated by Brazil's CVM?

There is no published registration with the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM), Brazil's securities regulator. The companies behind Safirion are registered in Nevis and Cyprus, not in Brazil, so the operation is not under Brazilian supervision or covered by local complaint channels. That's the point to understand before depositing.

See what changes without CVM registration
16Do I have to pay income tax in Brazil on what I earn?

Yes. Gains made at a broker abroad are taxable income and must be declared to the Receita Federal, Brazil's federal tax authority, like any other income, with the tax paid by you. The broker does not withhold the tax or issue a Brazilian tax statement: your trading history is the basis for the calculation.

See how to declare a broker abroad
17How does the DARF for day trading work?

The DARF is the Brazilian federal tax payment slip you use to pay the tax on the profit you calculate, and for day trading the payment is monthly, on the month's result. Missing the deadline brings a fine and interest, so keeping track month by month is worth more than sorting everything out in April.

See how to calculate and issue the DARF
18Is trading binary options a crime in Brazil?

Trading is not a crime for individuals, but offering the product in Brazil without authorization from the CVM, Brazil's securities regulator, is irregular, and that's where the difference lies. The practical consequence is that you trade without the protection an authorized institution would give you.

See what the rules say, in plain language
19What does Reclame Aqui, the Brazilian complaints site, show about Safirion?

A complaints page is a clue, not a verdict: it shows what keeps repeating in the reports — slow withdrawals, unexpected charges, trouble getting in touch — and is not meant for drawing conclusions on its own. What matters is the pattern of the complaints and the response given to them.

See how to read a complaints page
20How do the dollar account and currency conversion work?

The account is in US dollars, so the exchange rate decides how many Brazilian reais you put in when you deposit and how many you get back when you withdraw. You can end up with a profit in dollars and fewer reais than you expected, or the other way around: it's a risk separate from the market you trade.

See how the exchange rate moves your balance
21What are the official Instagram and Telegram accounts?

The official profiles are the ones Safirion itself lists on safirion.com, and checking there before following any invitation is what keeps you from getting scammed. A profile that promises paid signals, a private group or management of your account is not the broker's support.

See how to identify the official channels
22Safarion, Safirium, Safrion: what's the right name?

The right name is Safirion, and the variations show up because many people type it by ear when searching. This matters for security: a domain that looks like the official one is the route most used by fake pages, so check the spelling before you type your password.
